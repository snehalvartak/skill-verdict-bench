Do targeted risk checks catch malicious agent skills?

Experiment 004 scores SkillScreen's held-out test sets with the decision model clef-flash. It compares five targeted per-risk questions (combined three ways) against one generic "is this malicious?" question, and places both next to methods SkillScreen published.

What we found

  1. Targeted checks beat one generic question on both held-out test sets (pre-registered P1 and P2): AUROC +0.139 [+0.105, +0.172] on source-disjoint skills and +0.129 [+0.105, +0.152] on structurally disjoint ones. On sources never seen before, the generic question is at chance (0.518).
  2. Learned weights don't transfer to new sources. Taking the max of the five checks is as good as the fitted logistic regression on structurally disjoint skills and slightly better on source-disjoint ones (−0.014 [−0.027, −0.002]), unlike experiment 003.
  3. SkillHarm is the blind spot. At the 5% false-positive threshold the checks catch 85 of 90 SkillFortifyBench and 18 of 21 ATR malicious skills, but 39 of 728 SkillHarm ones. Even at SkillScreen's own false-positive rate they catch about half of SkillHarm, against SkillScreen's 95%.
  4. Where it stands: level with TypeSafe Jev 1.13, well behind SkillScreen's fine-tuned model (0.903 and 0.992 AUROC) and GPT-5.5 as a judge (0.914 and 0.950), but with far fewer false positives at a strict threshold.

Full write-up, with every pre-registered comparison and the limitations: docs/experiments/004-skillscreen-cohorts.md. Run: 9,842 predictions, $2.01, about 72 minutes.

How to read this

  • Pre-registered: the headline differences (P1 on source-disjoint, P2 on structurally disjoint), the combiners and the validation thresholds. The source chart at a moving threshold, and any threshold you pick here, are exploratory.
  • Combiners: logreg is a logistic regression on the five check scores fitted on 300 training skills; max takes the highest check; AST04 uses one check alone; generic-1q is the single generic question.
  • Model: hosted cloudflare/clef-flash through OpenRouter, served by Cloudflare only. OpenRouter returned no dated version, so a silent model change during the run would not be detected.
  • Unscored: one structurally disjoint test skill is over the model's 24,576-token context and has no score; it is left out of the curves.
  • SkillScreen's methods are different models scored by their authors; comparisons with them are descriptive only.
